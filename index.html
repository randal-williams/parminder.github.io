About

I am a UK and European patent attorney in Cambridge, specialising in artificial intelligence, software, bioinformatics and physics-based inventions. Alongside my patent practice, I speak and write regularly on how AI is changing innovation and the patent profession itself, and have served on the Council of the Chartered Institute of Patent Attorneys (CIPA). I am currently Vice-Chair of CIPA's AI Committee, and a member of the AI Special Interest Group of CWConnected (formerly Cambridge Wireless).

I trained as a physicist, first completing an MSci at Imperial College London and then a DPhil at the University of Oxford. My doctoral thesis described my research on constructing a synthetic ribosome using synthetic DNA as the building material. This multi-disciplinary background enables me to handle a wide variety of innovations including bioinformatics and techbio. I have worked in the patent profession for over 15 years, with experience in both private practice and in-house.

My contributions to the IP profession include widening access to the profession and helping those who join as trainee patent attorneys to succeed. I jointly led the team that created IP Inclusive's Careers in Ideas outreach programme, and contributed to CIPA's Education Committee with a particular focus on helping trainees through their qualifying exams. I also routinely mentor start-up founders and provide training to my clients to help them better understand which of their software innovations are more likely to be patentable. I have also delivered presentations on protecting innovation using IP more widely, including to members of the Institute of Physics, the Royal Society of Chemistry, the Association of Anaesthetists and CWConnected, and to a variety of universities. My particular skill is explaining complex legal issues to people who have no reason to know how the law works.

Speaking

I speak at conferences, webinars, and training days, for specialist and general audiences. Recurring topics:

Patenting AI and software

What makes an AI invention "technical", and the differing challenges to patent software and AI in the UK, Europe and US.

AI for busy professionals

What AI is, what it can do, and how it is already used in medicine, chemistry and law, without the jargon.

AI and the IP profession

Generative AI tools in patent work: risks to confidentiality and precision, and what to consider when developing or buying a legal AI tool.

IP for start-ups and deeptech

Building a competitive advantage from patents and trade secrets, for founders, spin-outs and early-stage tech companies.

Careers, diversity and inclusion

Life after qualification, allyship, intersectionality and wellbeing in the professions.
